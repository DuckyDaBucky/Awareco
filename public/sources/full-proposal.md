# Make A Mark Live — Full Proposal

Oct 20, 2026 · @Hamiz Iqbal

## The proposal on one page

**Make A Mark Live** is an AI impact engine, built and run by Cognizant, that turns Aston Martin Aramco F1's (AMF1's) sustainability, inclusion and community data into a trusted, live view of impact. Fans and partners explore it through three spaces, guided by an AI version of Fernando Alonso.

**Pitch line:** "Make A Mark Live turns AMF1's impact data into something fans can explore, partners can trust, and the team can earn from."

**What it is**

- **The engine (the AI work):** verifies AMF1's data from source records, explains it with cited answers, and simulates "what if" decisions. Every number traces back to a source.
- **AI-lonso, the front door:** the team's existing AI Alonso voice persona, revived as a guide that answers questions and matches fans to the people and programmes behind the team.
- **Three spaces:**
  - **The Car:** the engineering and sustainability explorer, plus a live race-weekend impact tile.
  - **The People:** community and inclusion stories told by personas grounded in real programmes.
  - **The Partners:** a professional dashboard for charities and sponsors, plus an internal inclusion view.
- **The loop:** AMF1 social posts bring fans in, fans engage and act, and their actions become measurable impact. The team's mission says it measures success partly by "our ability to encourage others to do the same."

**Why it should win**

- It answers both halves of the challenge. The AI engine makes the data trusted and live, and the three spaces deliver it in creative, F1-native ways.
- It covers all three Make A Mark pillars: Environment, Belong and Community.
- It's grounded in AMF1's own written goals: "robust data systems" and "strengthen the quality and integrity of our data."
- It makes money. The leading options are licensing the engine as a Cognizant product, paid fan experiences (including a race-day booth), and new partner activation.
- It's realistic: about 9 months to full launch with a Cognizant team of about 8. The year-1 investment is about $1.13M, run costs about $230K a year after that, and the modelled payback lands in year 3. All financials are estimates to validate.

**The ask:** approve a 4-month pilot at about $436K, launching The Car and AI-lonso at one race weekend in 2027. Scale to all three spaces, with a race-day booth at the 2027 United States GP in Austin.

## The problem

AMF1 does real, measurable good, but fans, partners and even the team itself see it as a PDF published every one to two years.

**For AMF1 (the ESG team)**

- **Slow:** progress is reported "at least every two years" (2025 report). The DE&I survey runs every two years.
- **Incomplete:** both reports list topics as "material but information currently unavailable", including supplier environmental and social assessment, biodiversity, materials and non-discrimination. Emissions intensity is "not available".
- **Estimated:** the supply chain is about 80% of emissions and is mostly estimated from spend.
- **Hard to keep consistent:** the 2023 baseline appears as 94,253 and as 97,216 tCO₂e across documents (restated after SBTi validation). The "laps of Silverstone" equivalences differ by about 14× between the 2024 and 2025 reports.
- **They've asked for a fix:** "Develop robust data systems to track our progress and meet stakeholder expectations" (2024 report). "Strengthen the quality and integrity of our data" (ESG Director, 2025).

**For fans**

- Fans already care: ESG posts drew 144.8M impressions in 2024, 3× a typical race weekend, with 33% more interactions per post than 2023.
- But what they get is one-off posts. There's nothing to explore, question, personalise or act on.
- Fans who love the cars don't see how a 2026 car's 50:50 hybrid power unit and 100% sustainable fuel connect to the team's impact.

**For partners and charities**

- AMF1 co-runs most programmes with partners (Arm, Aramco, Maaden, Valvoline, Xerox, Cognizant) and charities (Aleto, AFBE-UK, Racing Pride, Movember and others).
- Outcomes are mostly event counts and self-reported surveys. There's no shared, verified view of what each partnership achieved, which makes renewing and expanding partnerships harder.

## The impact engine

The engine is where the AI does its work. It turns raw records into a verified impact ledger, answers questions only from that ledger, and runs what-if scenarios on it. AI never invents a number.

**Data in, at three speeds**

| Speed | Sources | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live (minutes to daily) | Campus building management system and energy meters, on-site solar output, freight and sustainable aviation fuel bookings (official logistics partner Atlas Air), travel bookings, event and booth sign-ups, website and app engagement | AMF1 facilities, logistics, digital |
| Monthly / quarterly | Supplier invoices and product data, HR snapshots (aggregated only), mentoring and event surveys, charity and match-funding logs | AMF1 procurement, HR, ESG; charity partners |
| Annual (audited) | The independently assured carbon footprint (ISO 14064-3), SBTi targets, gender pay gap report | AMF1 ESG team |

**Job 1: Verify (raw data → trusted ledger)**

1. Ingest each source through automated pipelines.
2. AI reads unstructured inputs (supplier invoices, product data sheets, event write-ups, survey text) and extracts the values.
3. AI classifies each value into a pillar and metric: Environment (emissions by scope, energy, waste, circularity), Belong (workforce mix, programmes), Community (reach, outcomes, funds).
4. Rules check each value against history, the 2023 baseline and the 2030 targets. Anomalies and gaps go to a human reviewer.
5. Each approved value is stored with its source, date, method, version and confidence. Restated baselines keep both versions.

**Job 2: Explain (ledger → insights)**

- AI-lonso and the personas answer through retrieval from the verified ledger only, and every answer shows its source.
- Equivalences (laps of Silverstone, cars off the road, race weekends) are calculated in code from fixed, cited conversion factors (UK DEFRA, US EPA). The AI only writes the sentence around them.
- The engine spots trends on its own. Example: "Travel emissions are down 14%, but commuting is now 7% of the footprint."
- If the ledger has no answer, the AI says so and links to the official page.

**Job 3: Simulate (insights → decisions)**

- What-if scenarios built on AMF1's own figures, shown as ranges with editable assumptions.
- Example: "What if HQ ran on 100% on-site solar?" HQ energy is under 1% of emissions and the supply chain is about 80%, so it barely moves the total. Supplier engagement does.
- Example: "What if STEM outreach funding rose 20%?" From 257 students and 14 schools at Make A Mark Day 2025 → roughly 300–310 students, 2–3 more schools, assuming cost per student stays flat.
- Example: "What if half of next season's fly-away freight moved from air to sea?" Uses the team's air-to-sea history (−1,500 tCO₂e in 2022–23, −290 tCO₂e in 2023–24).
- Partners use the same simulator to test proposed initiatives (section 9).

**Technology (all delivered and run by Cognizant)**

- A governed cloud data platform with data lineage (the verified ledger).
- Enterprise generative AI for extraction, classification and retrieval-based answers, with guardrails.
- A calculation service for equivalences and simulations.
- A responsive web experience embedded in AMF1's website and app, with a partner dashboard.
- AMF1 owns its data; Cognizant operates the platform under a managed-service agreement.

## AI-lonso: the front door

AI-lonso is AMF1's existing AI Alonso voice persona. It was launched in October 2024 to read and translate team website content in an AI version of Alonso's voice, then saw little use. We revive it as the guide to the whole experience.

**The home page**

- Centre: a large AI-lonso tile. Ask anything, or pick a suggested prompt.
- Left: **The People** (community and inclusion).
- Right: **The Car** (engineering).
- Bottom: **Sustainability**, the live impact view and race tile.
- Navigation: no hard nav bar, just the arrow inside the AMF1 logo, centred on every page.
- Visual style: deep AMOLED black with AMF1 racing green, a thin white grid in the background, minimal and premium.

**What AI-lonso does**

- Answers questions from the verified ledger, with a source on every answer.
- Speaks English, Spanish and French at launch, matching the original feature, with more languages later.
- Navigates: "Show me how the 2026 power unit works" opens that part in The Car.
- Suggests prompts: "What did the team achieve this year?", "Match me with someone interesting", "What if the team went all-in on sustainable fuel?"
- Runs the fan "make your call" simulator. Fans pick where the team should invest next, and every choice is counted (anonymously) as engagement data.

**Matchmaking ("Match me")**

- AI-lonso asks 3–4 light questions about **interests and goals**: "Do you like building things, data, or people?", "Are you a student, working, or just a fan?", "Which cause matters most to you?"
- It matches the fan to a persona and programme, for example a future engineer to the Aleto mentoring story, or a climate-minded fan to the circular car story.
- It never asks about or stores sensitive traits (sexuality, ethnicity, disability, health). Fans can explore any community's story freely without declaring anything.
- Each match ends with a real next step: read the programme page, sign up for a session, share, or book an experience.

**Guardrails**

- AI-lonso is clearly labelled as AI.
- It uses only content approved by Alonso and the team, with no off-topic or opinion answers.
- It is never used for anything Alonso hasn't approved.

## Space 1: The Car (engineering and sustainability)

The Car shows fans how engineering choices create sustainability impact, part by part and generation by generation, with each figure pulled live from the engine.

**Page 1: Component explorer (top-down view)**

- An aerial view of the current car with tappable zones: power unit (engine and hybrid system), energy store (battery), tyres, front and rear wings, chassis and bodywork, fuel.
- Tapping a zone opens a panel:
  - Header: the component name.
  - Right: an animated image of the part.
  - Left: a plain-English explanation of what it does and how it got more sustainable.
  - Bottom: charts from the ledger, for example material circularity (32% for the AMR25 under the FIA Circularity Handbook), recycled carbon fibre (1.3 t in 2025), and the fuel and power-unit story.
- **Back in time:** swap a part for its earlier version to compare. Example: the 2026 power unit is a 1.6-litre V6 turbo hybrid with a roughly 50:50 split between electric and combustion power, running on 100% sustainable fuel developed with Aramco. This answers the fan question "why did engines change?"

**Page 2: Evolution slider (double-tap the car)**

- A side-profile car drives along the top. A year slider underneath morphs it through generations.
- Range: Aston Martin's first F1 era (1959–60) through today, focusing on the modern team (2021–2026). Earlier mock-ups showed 1960–2025; set the final range from the team's archive.
- Each stop shows a sustainability milestone, for example campus solar, the first sustainable aviation fuel purchase (2025), and the 2026 sustainable-fuel regulations.

**Live race tile (on the home page, during race weekends)**

- Shows that weekend's impact against **AMF1's own baseline and targets**: freight mode and sustainable aviation fuel share, trackside energy mix (renewable diesel generators vs renewable grid vs solar, using F1-provided trackside data), booth and fan actions.
- Example: "This weekend: renewable-backed paddock energy, freight emissions 31% below a conventional air plan."
- **We don't claim superiority over other teams.** No public per-team, per-race emissions data exists, and an unsupported claim invites greenwashing criticism. We compare AMF1 to its own targets and to F1-wide programmes it takes part in.

**Bridge to Space 2: "Meet the minds behind the machine"**

Every part was designed by people. From any component, tap the engineer behind it (for example, the composite design team behind the para-canoe seat project). That leads straight into the programmes that develop such people, like Aleto mentoring and Accelerate Women, in The People.

## Space 3: The Partners (charities and sponsors)

The Partners is the professional, data-dense version of The People, made for charities, sponsors and AMF1 leadership. It proves what each partnership achieved and helps plan the next one. It's also where most of the revenue comes from.

**Who uses it**

- **Charity partners** (Aleto, AFBE-UK, Racing Pride, Movember, Spinal Track, Grand Prix Trust and others): see the reach and outcomes of joint programmes, and get board-ready impact reports.
- **Commercial partners** (Aramco, Maaden, Arm, Atlas Air, Valvoline, Xerox, Cognizant and others): see verified impact from their activations, for example the outcomes of Unearth Your Greatness, or the freight emissions avoided with sustainable aviation fuel.
- **AMF1 leadership and the ESG team:** one live view of all three pillars against targets, with draft content for CDP, SBTi and GRI reporting.

**Features**

- A professional assistant (no cartoon persona) that gives concise, data-heavy answers with sources. Example: "Cost per student reached across 2024–25 outreach, by programme."
- Business-intelligence dashboards: outcomes over time, by programme, by partner and by race location.
- **Initiative simulator:** test a proposed programme before committing. Example: "If we co-fund a second Aleto cohort in the US, what reach and cost per outcome should we expect?" Results show as ranges, using the evidence from past cohorts.
- One-click exportable impact reports, with partner branding, for renewals and board meetings.
- Gap tracker: shows each "information currently unavailable" topic from the reports, with its owner and data-collection status.

**Internal inclusion view (staff and leadership only, never public)**

- Women by function: 48% of enabling functions, 5% of operations, 8% of race team, 11% of technical roles, 0% of board directors (2025).
- Gender pay gap trend: 20.6% median, 54.3% mean (April 2025).
- Training hours (0.5 per person) against 539 available modules; hiring (26.2%) and turnover (18.3%).
- Employee group membership and survey results.
- Simulator use: "If 15% of operations hires were women, how would representation and the pay gap change over 3 years?"
- Privacy: aggregated data only, minimum group sizes so no one can be identified, access limited to HR and leadership roles, and compliance with UK GDPR.

**Future extensions (to be confirmed)**

- Custom partner events planned and measured in the platform (for example a co-branded STEM day), with impact tracked from sign-up to outcome.
- Shared impact reporting with F1 and the FIA ESG working group.

## The engagement-to-impact loop

Engagement is the engine's fuel. AMF1's social channels bring fans in, the spaces turn attention into action, and each action becomes measurable impact that the team can show back to fans and partners.

**The loop**

1. **Reach:** an AMF1 Instagram story or post, tied to a moment (race weekend, Pride Month, International Women's Day, Make A Mark Week), links to a space.
2. **Engage:** the fan explores The Car, talks to a persona, or asks AI-lonso.
3. **Personalise:** "Match me" connects them to a programme or story that fits their interests.
4. **Act:** share, sign up, apply, donate, make their call in the simulator, or book an experience.
5. **Measure:** the engine counts each action and links it to the programme it supports.
6. **Show impact:** the race tile and partner reports show the results, which feed the next post.

## Risks, mitigations and responsible AI

The risks that matter most are wrong numbers, greenwashing, privacy and the use of a real driver's likeness. Each one is designed out, not just monitored.

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI invents or misstates a figure | Medium | High | Answers only from the verified ledger; source on every answer; equivalences in code; "I don't know" fallback; accuracy tests before every release | Cognizant AI engineer + AMF1 product owner |
| Greenwashing accusation | Medium | High | Show gaps as well as wins (e.g. supply chain only about 9% down); no comparisons with other teams; numbers reconcile with the audited footprint; ESG team approves fan content | AMF1 ESG Director |
| Inclusion data exposes individuals | Low | High | Aggregated only; minimum group sizes; internal view restricted to HR and leadership; UK GDPR assessment in Phase 0 | AMF1 legal / HR |
| AI-lonso misuse of Alonso's likeness | Low | High | Alonso and team approve the voice, scope and scripts; labelled as AI; narrow topic guardrails | AMF1 marketing + legal |
| Personas misrepresent real people or communities | Medium | Medium | Labelled composites; partner charity approves each one; real quotes only with consent | AMF1 + charity partners |
| Data arrives late or patchy (suppliers, partners) | High | Medium | Start with live feeds the team already has; supplier engagement builds on the 70% already engaged; gaps shown openly | AMF1 procurement |
| Low fan adoption | Medium | Medium | Launch on race weekends through AMF1's own social channels; AI-lonso and booth as hooks; pilot gate before scaling | AMF1 digital |
| Activation slots don't sell | Medium | Medium | Sign one partner before scaling; booth and experiences revenue don't depend on sponsors | AMF1 partnerships |
| Timeline slips | Medium | Low | Phase gates; scope fixed per phase; pilot limited to one space | Cognizant delivery lead |
| Security breach | Low | High | Enterprise security, role-based access, encryption, penetration testing before launch | Cognizant architect |

**Responsible-AI principles (one line for the deck)**

"Every number traceable, every scenario explained, no individual exposed, and AI always labelled as AI."

- **Transparency:** sources and assumptions are always visible.
- **Fairness:** the simulator shows ranges and editable assumptions; humans make decisions.
- **Privacy:** consent first; aggregated inclusion data; no sensitive traits collected in matchmaking.
- **Accountability:** named human owners approve every published figure and persona.
